How to Take a Good Plant Photo

AI diagnostic accuracy relies on sharp image details. Follow these simple smartphone photography practices to ensure reliable disease and vigor assessments.

Ready to Analyze
Best Practices

Key Rules for Accurate Plant AI Scans

  • Focus Directly on the Leaf Surface:

    Tap your smartphone screen on the lesion or discoloration to lock focal sharpness before taking the shot.

  • Use Diffuse Natural Daylight:

    Early morning or late afternoon natural daylight is ideal. Avoid harsh direct flashlight which causes washed-out white spots.

  • Frame Both Symptoms and Healthy Tissue:

    Showing a bit of normal green leaf alongside the diseased brown/yellow area helps the computer vision model calibrate color contrast.

Photography Do's and Don'ts

DO
  • Get within 15–30 cm of the leaf: Fill at least 60% of the camera frame with the affected foliage.
  • Photograph the leaf underside if rust or aphids are suspected: Pustules and colonies reside beneath the leaf cuticle.
  • Hold the phone steady with both hands: Even minor motion blur degrades edge detection and lesion contour identification.
  • Keep the background neutral: Use your hand or clean ground behind the leaf to prevent background foliage distraction.
DON'T
  • Don't take wide-angle photos of an entire field: A distant photo of 50 plants cannot reveal microscopic fungal spots.
  • Don't upload dark or night photos taken without proper illumination: Dark photos trigger the blur and low-exposure guardrail.
  • Don't capture through dirty greenhouse glass or mesh: Reflections and grids distort morphological texture metrics.
  • Don't upload non-plant objects: The built-in chlorophyll and texture filter will reject non-botanical images.
