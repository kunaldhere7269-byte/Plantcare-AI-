Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common technical, agronomic, and project questions regarding PlantCare AI and our college Community Engagement Project.

PlantCare AI is an applied web-based agricultural intelligence system developed as part of our college Community Engagement Project (CEP). Its primary goal is to address agricultural and horticultural challenges faced by smallholder farmers, local community gardeners, and botany students by democratizing access to automated foliar pathology screening and integrated pest management (IPM) guidance.

When an image is submitted, the system executes a dual-phase analysis:
  1. Pre-processing & Computer Vision: Using Pillow and OpenCV, the system evaluates image clarity (Laplacian blur variance), verifies chlorophyll coloration (Excess Green index), and detects necrotic/chlorotic pixel distributions.
  2. Multimodal Vision AI / Expert Rules: If configured with a GEMINI_API_KEY, Google Gemini's multimodal vision model analyzes leaf morphology, lesion contours, and pathogen signs. If offline or unconfigured, the transparent Offline Heuristic Engine evaluates visual ratios against an agricultural pathology rule base.

A 2D smartphone photograph without physical reference calibration cannot scientifically measure subterranean soil chemistry, electrical conductivity (EC), exact soil moisture content, or millimeter plant height. The system strictly distinguishes visible observations (leaf discoloration, lesions, turgor) from factors that require physical user input or field sensors.

All uploaded photographs, thumbnails, and analysis records are stored strictly in your local uploads/ folder and local SQLite database (data/plantcare.db). No personal data or user images are sent to third-party tracking services or commercial advertising brokers.

Overuse of synthetic chemical pesticides causes severe environmental problems, water contamination, beneficial pollinator loss, and pathogen resistance. PlantCare AI follows an IPM-first philosophy: recommending cultural prevention (proper spacing, straw mulching, drip irrigation) and biological treatments (neem oil, Bacillus subtilis, copper soaps) before synthetic chemicals.
